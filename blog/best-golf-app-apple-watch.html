Best Golf App for Apple Watch in 2026: Ranked

Golfshot, 18Birdies, Hole19 and Arccos on the Wrist — What Each Does and Costs

August 29, 2026 · 8 min read · Stephen Pickering

Key takeaway: Golfshot has the best overall Apple Watch experience (its top watch features need Pro at $59.99/year); 18Birdies is the best free watch app, with GPS, scoring, and stats on the wrist for nothing. Hole19 Premium (~$40/year) is the value pick for plays-like distances. Budget a third of your watch battery per 18 holes — and remember no watch app structures your practice.

The best golf app for Apple Watch in 2026 is Golfshot if you’ll pay for Pro, and 18Birdies if you want the best free watch app. Hole19 is the value pick for plays-like distances, and Arccos suits data golfers already invested in its sensors.

The appeal of a watch app is obvious: yardages and scoring on your wrist, phone in the bag, no fumbling on the tee. But watch apps vary enormously in what the free tier actually gives you, how much battery a round costs, and whether the wrist features are the product or a paywalled extra. Here’s how the four best options compare — and the one thing none of them can do.

The Best Golf Apps for Apple Watch, Ranked

1. Golfshot — best overall watch experience

Golfshot’s Apple Watch app is one of its strongest features: fast distances on the wrist, scoring without touching your phone, and an interface that stays stable through a full round. For golfers who genuinely want to leave the phone in the bag, it’s the most complete wrist experience available.

The catch is the paywall. The free tier covers basic GPS and scoring, but the best watch functionality — along with advanced GPS, club tracking, and the Caddie 2.0 AI recommendations — sits in Pro at $59.99/year. There’s a 14-day free trial, which is exactly the right way to find out whether you’d actually use it.

Best for: golfers who hate carrying a phone and will pay for the privilege.

2. 18Birdies — best free Apple Watch golf app

18Birdies includes Apple Watch support in its free tier — and that free tier is already the most generous in the category. From the wrist you get GPS yardages, score entry, and basic stat tracking; on the phone it adds front/centre/back distances, a polished scorecard, and the social features that make it the default app for weekend groups.

The watch app is functional rather than fancy, and battery drain is real: expect to lose 30–40% of watch charge over 18 holes. But as a free wrist setup, nothing else matches it. Premium ($99.99/year) adds the AI caddie and advanced stats, none of which you need for the watch basics.

Best for: golfers who want wrist yardages and scoring without paying anything.

3. Hole19 — best value paid option

Hole19’s watch app covers the essentials — GPS distances and scoring from the wrist — with a free tier that’s genuinely usable. Premium, at roughly $40/year, is where it gets interesting: plays-like distances that account for elevation, club recommendations built from your real carry numbers, and Otto, its AI caddie. That’s meaningfully cheaper than Golfshot Pro for a comparable on-course feature set.

Best for: golfers who want plays-like distances on a budget.

4. Arccos — best for shot-tracking data

Arccos is a different animal: a sensor-based shot-tracking system first, a watch app second. With the grip sensors fitted (~$200, plus a subscription around $99/year), the Apple Watch becomes a companion screen for distances and caddie advice while the sensors capture every shot automatically for strokes gained analysis.

If you’re not buying into the whole Arccos ecosystem, it’s not the watch pick. If you are, the watch is a genuinely useful part of it.

Best for: data golfers already committed to sensor tracking.

Which one should you choose?

Free and simple: 18Birdies. Best wrist experience and money’s no object: Golfshot Pro. Plays-like intelligence for less: Hole19 Premium. Full shot data: Arccos.

Honestly, for most golfers the free 18Birdies watch app plus a full battery is 90% of what the paid options deliver on the course.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best golf app for Apple Watch?

Golfshot has the strongest overall watch experience — fast yardages, wrist scoring, and a stable interface — though its best watch features sit in the $59.99/year Pro tier. For a free watch app, 18Birdies is the pick: yardages, score entry, and basic stats without paying anything.

Is there a free golf app for Apple Watch?

Yes. 18Birdies includes Apple Watch support in its free tier — GPS yardages, score entry, and basic stats from the wrist. Hole19’s free tier also covers watch GPS and scoring. Golfshot’s watch experience is stronger, but its best features need Pro.

How much battery does a golf app use on Apple Watch?

Expect to lose roughly a third of your watch battery over 18 holes with GPS running — 18Birdies typically costs 30–40% of charge per round. Start at full charge, and on older watches turn off the always-on display for the round.

Can you practise golf with an Apple Watch app?

Not really. Watch golf apps are built for the course — yardages, scoring, shot tracking. None of them structure practice. Improvement comes from scored, benchmarked practice between rounds, which is a phone-and-practice-green job rather than a wrist one.
