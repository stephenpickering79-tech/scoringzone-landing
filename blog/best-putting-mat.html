Best Putting Mat 2026: 5 Ranked

Wellputt, PuttOut, SKLZ and More — Plus the Drills That Make a Mat Worth It

August 31, 2026 · 9 min read · Stephen Pickering

Key takeaway: The Wellputt is the best putting mat overall — true roll, realistic speed, and a drill system printed on the surface. PuttOut is the premium compact pick, SKLZ Accelerator the budget one. Buy at least 8 feet (10–12 is better) at stimp 10–11 — then run scored drills on it, because the mat alone fixes nothing.

The best putting mat for most golfers is the Wellputt — a true-rolling surface at realistic green speed with a printed drill system on the mat itself. The PuttOut mat is the best premium compact pick, and the SKLZ Accelerator is the budget option that still rolls honestly.

But here’s the thing most putting mat reviews skip: the mat is the cheap part. The average 15+ handicapper three-putts over three times a round, and no surface fixes that on its own — scored practice does. This guide ranks the five mats worth buying, the four specs that actually matter, and the drills that turn ten minutes on a mat into fewer putts on the course.

What Actually Matters in a Putting Mat

Four specs separate a training surface from a novelty:

Length. At least 8 feet; 10–12 is better. Most knee-knockers on the course are 3–8 feet, and a longer mat lets you ladder between distances instead of grooving one putt.

Speed. Look for a listed stimp around 10–11 — normal green speed. A thin, glassy mat that runs at 13+ teaches a tentative stroke that falls apart on real greens.

A true roll. The surface must be flat and consistent. If the ball wobbles offline on a dead-straight strike, the feedback loop the mat exists for is broken.

A return or target system. Not essential, but a ball return or marked target zones keeps the reps flowing — and rep volume is the whole point of practising at home.

The Best Putting Mats, Ranked

1. Wellputt — best overall

Wellputt mats roll true at a realistic speed, come in lengths from 10 to 26 feet, and print the practice system directly on the mat — target zones, gates, and distance markers that pair with a companion app of structured drills. It’s the mat that treats putting practice as training rather than killing time, which is exactly the right mindset. Expect to pay around $130–$200 depending on length.

Best for: golfers who want structured practice built into the surface.

2. PuttOut mat — best premium compact

PuttOut made its name with the Pressure Putt Trainer — the curved-ramp target that returns good putts and rejects weak ones — and its mat is built to pair with it: a dense, true-rolling surface with alignment markings and distance dots. The mat-plus-trainer combination gives you a hole-sized target with real consequences in about seven feet of hallway. Around $100 for the mat; the trainer adds roughly $35.

Best for: small spaces and pressure practice with the Pressure Putt Trainer.

3. SKLZ Accelerator — best budget pick

The SKLZ Accelerator Pro is the honest budget option: about 9 feet long, a built-in ball return, and printed alignment guides at 3, 5, and 7 feet. The surface runs slightly quick and the up-slope into the cup isn’t how real holes work — but at around $40 it delivers the two things that matter most: repeatable distances and a target. If price is the barrier to practising at home, this removes it.

Best for: golfers who want reps without spending three figures.

4. Perfect Practice — best return system

The Perfect Practice mat — the one all over Tour players’ social feeds — has the most satisfying return system in the category: two hole sizes (regulation and a smaller “make it harder” cup) and a rail that feeds the ball back to your feet. The standard version runs around $160. The dual-hole design is genuinely useful: pass your drill on the small hole and the real one looks like a bucket.

Best for: high-volume short-putt reps with instant feedback.

5. Any flat carpet + a gate of tees — best free option

Honest inclusion: if the budget is zero, a flat carpet, two tees as a gate, and a coin as a target train stroke mechanics, start line, and face control — which is most of what indoor putting practice can improve anyway. The mat upgrades the feedback and the realism; it doesn’t create the improvement by itself.

Best for: proving you’ll actually practise before you spend anything.

The Drills That Make a Putting Mat Worth It

A mat plus aimless rolling is furniture. A mat plus scored drills is practice. Three to run, straight from the drills that transfer best:

10 in a row. From 5 feet (or your mat’s longest marked distance), make ten consecutive putts. Miss one, back to zero. This is the pressure drill — streaks make a 4-footer on the course feel familiar instead of frightening.

Speed ladder. On an 8-foot-plus mat, set targets at 3, 6, and 9 feet. Three balls to each in sequence, a point for each ball dead-weight at the target. Distance control is the hardest skill to train indoors; this is the drill that gets closest.

The gate. Two tees a putter-head-width apart, two feet in front of the ball. Twenty clean gates in a row before you finish. Face angle and path, trained simultaneously, no hole required.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best putting mat?

The Wellputt is the best overall — a true-rolling surface at realistic green speed with a printed drill system that structures your practice. The PuttOut mat is the best premium compact option, and the SKLZ Accelerator is the best budget pick with a built-in return. Whichever you choose, get at least 8 feet of length.

What should I look for in a putting mat?

Four things: length (at least 8 feet, ideally 10–12), a listed stimp speed around 10–11, a flat true-rolling surface, and ideally a return mechanism. A thin, glassy novelty mat builds habits that fight you on real greens.

Do putting mats actually improve your putting?

Yes — stroke mechanics, alignment, face control, and short-range confidence all transfer to the course. Distance control is harder to replicate indoors, but it’s not where most amateurs lose strokes. The key is scored drills with consequences, not just rolling balls.

How long should a putting mat be?

At least 8 feet, and 10–12 if you have the space. Most pressure putts on the course are 3–8 feet, and a longer mat lets you ladder between distances instead of grooving one length. Under 6 feet limits you to stroke mechanics only.
