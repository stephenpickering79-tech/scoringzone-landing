Arccos vs 18Birdies: Which Should You Actually Use?

Sensors and Strokes Gained vs Free GPS and Scoring — the 2026 Head-to-Head

August 29, 2026 · 9 min read · Stephen Pickering

Key takeaway: Arccos wins on data: automatic sensor tracking, strokes gained, and an AI caddie for ~$200 in hardware plus ~$99/year. 18Birdies wins on value: the most generous free tier in golf apps, with Premium at $99.99/year. They barely compete for the same golfer — and neither one structures the practice that fixes what they diagnose.

Quick verdict: Arccos wins on data — automatic sensor-based shot detection, proper strokes gained analysis, and an AI caddie — but costs around $200 in hardware plus roughly $99 a year. 18Birdies wins on value: the free tier’s GPS, scorecard, stats, and social features are the most generous in the category, with Premium at $99.99/year if you want the AI caddie and advanced breakdowns. They’re barely competing for the same golfer.

This Arccos vs 18Birdies comparison covers what each does well, what each actually costs, which type of golfer each one suits — and the gap both leave wide open once the round is over.

What Arccos Does Well (and Where It Falls Short)

The strengths: automatic tracking and strokes gained

Arccos is the most seamless shot-tracking system in golf. Screw the sensors into your grip ends, pair them with the app, and every shot is detected automatically as you play — no tagging, no mid-round button pressing. After a handful of rounds the picture gets sharp.

The strokes gained engine is where it earns its money. Your game is split into the same categories the tour uses — off the tee, approach, around the green, putting — and benchmarked against golfers at your handicap, so you can see precisely where the strokes are leaking. Caddie 2.0 layers AI club and target recommendations on top, built from your own historical dispersion.

Where it falls short

Cost is the obvious one: roughly $200 for the sensors, then a subscription of around $99 a year to keep the analytics. You’re near $300 in year one, and the meter keeps running.

The subtler one: Arccos is entirely passive. It watches you play and tells you where you lost strokes. It doesn’t give you a single structured drill or scored practice session to fix what it finds. It also occasionally misses the very shots that decide your score — short chips and putts — which need editing after the round.

What 18Birdies Does Well (and Where It Falls Short)

The strengths: the most generous free tier in golf apps

18Birdies gives you more for nothing than any comparable app: GPS yardages to the front, centre, and back of every green, a polished digital scorecard, basic post-round stats (fairways, greens in regulation, putts), Apple Watch support, and a genuinely fun social layer — group rounds, leaderboards, and side games. For a weekend fourball, it’s the easy recommendation.

Premium — $99.99/year, $19.99/month, or $7.99/week — removes ads and adds the AI caddie with club recommendations, green-reading slope data, advanced stat breakdowns, and unlimited scorecard history.

Where it falls short

Everything is manual. 18Birdies knows what you scored, but not how — no sensors means no automatic shot detection, so its stats are only as good as what you tap in between shots. The analytics, even on Premium, are a summary of outcomes rather than true strokes gained diagnosis. Serious data golfers outgrow it; social golfers never need to.

Arccos vs 18Birdies: Head-to-Head

The key differences at a glance

What it is Arccos: Sensor-based shot tracking and strokes gained analytics 18Birdies: Free GPS, scorecard, stats, and social golf app

Price Arccos: ~$200 sensors + ~$99/year subscription (~$300 first year) 18Birdies: Free tier; Premium $99.99/year, $19.99/month, or $7.99/week

Shot detection Arccos: Automatic via grip sensors — very reliable for full shots, occasionally misses short chips and putts 18Birdies: Manual — you enter scores and stats yourself

Strokes gained analysis Arccos: Yes — full category breakdown, benchmarked against handicap peers 18Birdies: No true strokes gained — FIR, GIR, and putts free; deeper breakdowns on Premium

AI features Arccos: Caddie 2.0 — club and target recommendations from your own data 18Birdies: Premium AI caddie with club recommendations and green slope data

GPS and scoring Arccos: Via phone app, built around tracking rather than scoring 18Birdies: Core strength — front/centre/back GPS and a polished scorecard, free

Social features Arccos: Minimal 18Birdies: Best in class — group rounds, leaderboards, side games

Practice tools Arccos: None 18Birdies: None

That last row is the one comparison articles keep skipping. Both apps live on the course. Neither one structures the practice that actually changes the numbers they report.

Which one should you choose?

If you’re a data golfer who plays 30+ rounds a year and will genuinely act on strokes gained numbers, Arccos justifies its cost. Nothing else makes diagnosis this effortless.

If you want a brilliant free on-course app — yardages, scoring, and something to make the Saturday fourball more fun — 18Birdies is the obvious pick, and most golfers never need to pay for Premium.

If you’re choosing between spending ~$300 on Arccos or nothing on 18Birdies purely to find out why you’re not improving, here’s the honest shortcut: the answer is almost always the short game, and neither app helps you fix it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Arccos better than 18Birdies?

They’re built for different golfers. Arccos is a serious shot-tracking system — automatic sensor detection, strokes gained analysis, an AI caddie — at roughly $200 in hardware plus around $99 a year. 18Birdies is a free GPS, scorecard, and social app with optional Premium at $99.99/year. Deep data: Arccos. Free and fun on the course: 18Birdies.

Does 18Birdies have strokes gained like Arccos?

Not at the same depth. 18Birdies Premium adds advanced breakdowns on top of the free tier’s fairways, greens, and putts, but everything is manually entered. Arccos captures every shot automatically and benchmarks you against your handicap peers across all four strokes gained categories.

Which is cheaper, Arccos or 18Birdies?

18Birdies, by a distance. The free tier costs nothing and covers GPS, scoring, basic stats, and the social features. Premium is $99.99/year or $19.99/month. Arccos runs about $200 in sensors plus around $99 a year — close to $300 in your first year.

Do Arccos or 18Birdies help you practise?

No. Both are round-tracking tools — they report what happened on the course and go quiet between rounds. Neither offers structured, scored practice to fix what they find. Pairing either with a dedicated practice app closes that loop: scored drills, benchmarks, and a Short Game Handicap that shows the leak actually shrinking.
