iPing App: Does It Still Work in 2026?

Still Free, Still Updated — and Restricted to Authorised PING Fitters

September 2, 2026 · 7 min read · Stephen Pickering

Key takeaway: The iPing app is still on the App Store, still free, and was updated on 29 April 2026 — but PING states it is intended for authorised retailers and club fitters only, and the cradle it needs goes through that same channel. That mismatch, not bad software, is why it sits at roughly 2.1 stars. It measures stroke mechanics; it was never built to tell you whether your putting is improving.

Short answer: the iPing app still exists, it is still free, and PING updated it as recently as April 2026. But it is almost certainly not the app you are looking for.

Most golfers searching for it remember the original — the free consumer putting app that launched in 2011, clipped onto your iPhone and told you about your stroke. What is on the App Store today is iPING 2.0, and PING’s own listing is blunt about who it is for: authorised retailers and club fitters. If you are a golfer at home wanting to measure your putting, you will download it, find you cannot use it, and add to the pile of one-star reviews. Here is exactly what changed, and where to go instead.

What the iPing App Is Now

Still live, but rebuilt for the fitting bay

The app is published by PING, Inc., sits in the Sports category, costs nothing, and shows a current version of 3.5.1 dated 29 April 2026. It first appeared on 18 June 2011. So this is not abandonware — someone at PING is still maintaining it.

What has changed is the audience. The listing describes iPING 2.0 as a tool that “provides authorized PING club fitters and retailers a precise and easy-to-use tool to ensure golfers experience a tour-level putter fitting”, and then says it plainly further down: at this time the app is intended for use by authorised PING retailers and club fitters only, with accessories and technical support available to those fitters rather than to the public.

That last part is the bit that catches people. The app reads your stroke through the iPING cradle, a clip that holds the phone on the putter shaft. No cradle, no data. And the cradle is now routed through the fitter channel.

What it measures

Give it a cradle and a fitter, and the technology is genuinely good. It captures five metrics through the stroke:

  • Closing angle — how much the face rotates through the stroke, which classifies you as straight, slight arc or strong arc
  • Impact angle — where the face is pointing at the moment it meets the ball
  • Tempo — the ratio of your backstroke to your through-stroke
  • Lie angle — whether the putter is sitting toe-up or heel-up at impact
  • Shaft lean — forward or backward press through the ball

Those feed a fitting algorithm that recommends stroke type plus the length, loft, lie and weight of the putter that suits you. There are five modes, including Guided Fit, Single Putt and a Live Loft and Lie readout, all built around a shop workflow rather than a practice green.

Why it rates 2.1 stars

The US App Store has it at roughly 2.1 out of 5 from 94 ratings. That number looks damning and mostly is not about the software.

It is an expectations problem. Golfers go looking for a putting app they remember fondly from over a decade ago, find something with the same name, download it, and discover a fitting instrument they have no cradle for and no authorisation to use. That is a frustrating five minutes, and it ends in a one-star review. PING has not hidden any of this — it is in the listing — but nobody reads the listing before tapping Get on a free app.

What to Use Instead

Before picking a replacement, it is worth being honest about what you actually wanted, because two very different needs get bundled together here.

If you wanted your stroke measured, you want a current putting sensor. Capto and similar devices do what iPING did for consumers, they are still sold to the public, and they are not gated behind a fitter. Expect to pay for the privilege — this is proper hardware, not a free download.

If you wanted a putter fitted, book the fitting. That is what iPING 2.0 is for, and it works. Find an authorised PING fitter and you will get the whole thing done properly in half an hour, with the cradle in the fitter’s hands where PING intends it.

If what you really wanted was to know whether your putting is getting better — and for most golfers that is the honest answer — neither of the above does that. A perfect closing angle tells you nothing about whether you are holing more from six feet than you were a month ago.

Measure the outcome, not just the mechanics

Here is the thing about stroke data. Face angle and tempo are inputs. They matter, and a fitter using them to hand you the right putter is a good use of half an hour. But your scorecard does not record closing angle. It records putts.

Most golfers do not need more information about their stroke — they need to know whether the work is paying off. The average 15-plus handicapper three-putts more than three times a round. Fix that and you have found strokes no putter fitting will give you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the iPing app still available?

Yes. The PING app carrying iPING 2.0 is still on the App Store, still free, and was last updated on 29 April 2026. What has changed is who it is for: PING’s listing states the app is intended for authorised PING retailers and club fitters only, and that accessories and support go through those fitters rather than the public.

Why does the iPing app have such a low rating?

It sits at roughly 2.1 stars from 94 ratings on the US App Store, and most of that is a mismatch of expectations rather than broken software. Golfers remember the 2011-era consumer app, download it expecting a practice tool, and find a fitting tool built for a shop — one that needs the iPING cradle to read anything at all.

What does the iPing app actually measure?

Five stroke metrics: closing angle, impact angle, tempo, lie angle and shaft lean. Those feed PING’s fitting algorithm, which recommends stroke type plus putter length, loft, lie and weight. It is built to specify the right putter for you, not to score your practice or track whether you are improving.

What is the best alternative to the iPing app?

Depends what you were hoping for. If you want your stroke measured, a current sensor such as Capto does that job and is still sold to the public. If you want a putter fitted, book a fitting with an authorised PING fitter. And if what you actually wanted was to know whether your putting is improving, a scored practice app measures the outcomes — make percentage, lag proximity, three-putt avoidance — that decide your score.
