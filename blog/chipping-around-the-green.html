Chipping Around the Green: Shot Selection Made Simple

Read the Lie, Pick the Right Shot, and Get Up and Down More Often

July 14, 2026 · 7 min read · Stephen Pickering

Key takeaway: Most greenside shots are lost before the club moves — by picking the wrong shot. Read the lie first (it sets what’s possible), then follow the rule that saves the best players shots every round: putt when you can, chip when you can’t, flop only when you have no other option. Land the ball on a chosen spot rather than aiming at the hole, and match loft to how much green you have to work with. Right decision beats perfect technique every time.

Ask most golfers why they miss chances chipping around the green and they’ll blame their technique. Usually it’s the wrong culprit. The shot fell apart before they ever took the club back — because they picked the wrong shot for the situation.

Good greenside players aren’t magicians with the wedge. They’re just better at reading the lie, choosing the play with the most margin for error, and committing to it. Get that decision right and an average technique gets you up and down. Get it wrong and even a great strike finishes in the wrong place. Here’s how to make the decision simple every time.

Read the Lie Before You Pick the Shot

The lie decides what’s possible

Before you think about the hole, look at the ball. Sitting up on a tight fairway lie? You can play almost anything. Nestled down in thick rough? A delicate flop is off the table — the club will get grabbed and the shot won’t come out clean. The lie sets the menu. Ignore it and you’ll try shots the ground won’t let you hit.

Then look at what’s between you and the hole

Once you know what the lie allows, map the ground. How much fringe and rough do you have to carry? How much green is left for the ball to run on? Is there a slope that’ll feed the ball or fight it? You’re not aiming at the flag yet — you’re working out where the ball needs to land to end up beside it.

The One Rule That Simplifies Every Greenside Shot

Here’s the rule that quietly saves the best players shots every round: putt when you can, chip when you can’t, and only flop when you’ve no other option. The ball on the ground is almost always safer than the ball in the air. A putt from off the green can’t be chunked or bladed. A low bump-and-run has a huge margin for error. The high, spinning flop looks great on TV but brings every disaster back into play.

So work down the ladder. Can you putt it? Do that. If not, can you bump it low with a 9-iron or pitching wedge and let it release? Do that. Only when there’s rough to carry, an upslope to climb, or a pin you have to stop the ball quickly beside do you bring out the lofted wedge. Play the lowest-risk shot the situation allows and your up-and-down percentage climbs without touching your swing.

Land the Ball on a Spot, Not at the Hole

Pick a landing spot every time

Amateurs aim chips at the flag. Good players aim at a spot on the green where the ball will land and then roll out to the hole. It sounds like a small distinction — it’s the whole game. When you commit to a landing spot, your brain calibrates the swing to reach it, and the roll-out does the rest. Aiming at the hole gives your swing nothing specific to solve.

Match loft to how much green you have

The landing-spot idea also tells you which club to grab. Lots of green to work with? Take less loft, land it early, and let it run like a putt. Short-sided with the pin right on top of you? Take more loft, land it closer to the hole, and accept it’ll stop quickly. You’re not changing your technique between clubs — you’re changing the tool so the same simple swing produces a different amount of carry and roll. It’s worth spending a practice session hitting the same chip with three different clubs just to see how much the roll-out changes — it’s the fastest way to build a feel for club selection, and it’s the kind of decision-led practice Scoring Zone is built around rather than mindless repetition.

Practise the Decision, Not Just the Swing

The trouble with a bucket of balls to one flag is that it never trains the decision — the exact skill that decides whether you get up and down. Drop nine balls in nine different spots around the green instead, each with a different lie and angle, and play each one as a par 2: one chip, one putt, no gimmies. Now you’re rehearsing the read, the club choice, and the landing spot on every shot, which is what the course actually asks of you. Frequently Asked Questions

What club should I use for chipping around the green?

There’s no single right club. The more green you have between you and the hole, the more you want a lower-lofted club that rolls out like a putt. The less green you have, the more loft you need to carry the ball to the hole and stop it. A pitching wedge, a 9-iron, and a sand wedge cover almost every greenside shot you’ll face.

Should I chip or putt from around the green?

Putt whenever you can. If the ground between you and the green is smooth and you can roll the ball, a putt from off the green removes the risk of a fat or thin chip. Only reach for a chip when there’s rough, an upslope, or an obstacle you have to carry.

Why do I keep chunking chips around the green?

Chunking usually comes from hanging back on your trail foot and trying to lift the ball. Set most of your weight on your lead side, keep it there through the shot, and let the loft do the lifting. Ball-first contact fixes the fat shot.

How do I get up and down more often?

Choose the shot with the widest margin for error — usually the one that gets the ball rolling soonest — and land it on a spot you’ve picked, not at the hole. Then practise those shots with a score attached so you know your real up-and-down percentage and where it’s leaking.
